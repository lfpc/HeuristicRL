\author{Luis Felipe Cattelan}

\begin{abstract}
We compare eleven ways of using a hand-written heuristic policy to help PPO, from behaviour cloning with and
without a KL anchor, through DAgger, to three methods from the literature: HuRL (heuristic value as reward
shaping), HEPO (heuristic reward with a policy-improvement constraint) and HUBL (heuristic blending in offline
RL, here IQL). All are evaluated on four toy problems with known heuristics: online bin packing (with two different
guides), online knapsack, load balancing on heterogeneous servers and lost-sales inventory control, with 5 seeds
and 1.5M environment steps each. We ask whether a method (1)~ends better than RL from scratch, (2)~reaches the
heuristic's level faster than RL from scratch, and (3)~ends better than the heuristic. With this budget the answer to
(1) is almost always no: only IQL $\to$ PPO + KL (bin packing with the strong guide) and HuRL (load balancing)
are significantly better than PPO from scratch, and warm starts are sometimes significantly worse. The answer to (2)
is yes for nearly every method that uses the heuristic offline (IQL/HUBL and DAgger in all five experiments), as
long as the guide is beatable. The answer to (3) is yes for every warm-started method in every experiment, but PPO from scratch also
beats the heuristic in four of the five experiments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

\paragraph{Questions.} Given a decent heuristic policy $\pi_H$ for a sequential decision problem, which ways of
feeding it into reinforcement learning
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*}, leftmargin=*]
\item end \textbf{better than RL from scratch} (final metric of the trained policy, same environment budget)?
\item \textbf{reach the heuristic's level faster} than RL from scratch (environment steps until the policy first
  beats the heuristic, counting the heuristic's demonstration steps)?
\item end \textbf{better than the heuristic} itself?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Problems.} Four problems with fixed-length episodes, a discrete (masked) action space, a small vector
observation and a per-episode metric where lower is better (Table~\ref{tab:problems}). Each has a \emph{guide}
heuristic that the methods learn from and, where we know one, a stronger \emph{reference} heuristic that shows how
much room there is above the guide. Bin packing is run twice: guided by the weak Best Fit and by the near-optimal
Sum of Squares.

\section{Methods}

All methods share the same PPO: separate 2$\times$128 tanh MLPs for actor and critic, 16 parallel environments
$\times$ 128 steps per update, 4 epochs, minibatch 512, Adam $3\cdot10^{-4}$, $\gamma = 1$ (finite horizon), GAE
$\lambda = 0.95$, clip 0.2, entropy 0.01, value coefficient 0.5, gradient norm 0.5. The heuristic is a policy
$\pi_H(s)$, so the literature methods built around a heuristic reward or value were adapted as described.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{PPO from scratch.} The heuristic is not used.
\item \textbf{BC $\to$ PPO (naive).} Behaviour cloning on 64 heuristic episodes (cross-entropy, 10 epochs), then PPO
  from the cloned policy.
\item \textbf{BC $\to$ PPO + KL.} Same, but PPO adds $\beta_u\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{BC}})$ to the
  frozen clone, with $\beta$ decaying linearly from 1 to 0 by 50\% (or 20\%) of training, and the first 10 updates
  train only the critic.
\item \textbf{DAgger $\to$ PPO (naive / + KL).} The same 64-episode budget in 4 rounds: round 0 is the heuristic's
  own play, later rounds let the current policy act while the heuristic labels the visited states; retrain on the
  aggregate after each round. Then PPO as in 2 or 3.
\item \textbf{HuRL} (Cheng et al., 2021). A heuristic value $h(s)$ is fitted by least squares to the heuristic's
  Monte-Carlo returns on the demonstrations. PPO then trains on the reshaped problem
  $\tilde r = r + (1-\lambda_n)\gamma\, h(s')$, $\tilde\gamma = \lambda_n\gamma$, with
  $\lambda_n = \lambda_0 + (1-\lambda_0)\tanh(\omega n)$, $\lambda_0 = 0.9$, reaching $\approx 1$ at half of
  training. Run from scratch and from the BC policy (\textbf{BC $\to$ HuRL}).
\item \textbf{HEPO} (Lee et al., 2024). Maximise $(1+\alpha)J(\pi) + H(\pi)$ subject to $J(\pi) \ge J(\pi_H)$.
  Adaptation: the heuristic reward is $h(s,a) = 0.1\cdot\mathbb 1[a = \pi_H(s)]$, whose optimal policy is the
  heuristic itself, so $\pi_H$ is the heuristic (not trained). Half of the environments are played by $\pi_H$ on the
  same random sequences; $\pi$ is updated on both halves with advantage $(1+\alpha)A_r + A_h$ and
  $\pi_H(a\mid s) = 1$ in the importance ratio; $\alpha \leftarrow \max(0, \alpha - (\hat J(\pi) - \hat J(\pi_H)))$
  from Monte-Carlo returns.
\item \textbf{IQL $\to$ PPO + KL.} Offline RL on 64 $\varepsilon$-greedy heuristic episodes with
  $\varepsilon \in [0, 0.5]$ (mixed quality): discrete IQL (expectile 0.7, $\gamma = 0.99$, 10k updates) with an
  advantage-weighted actor ($\beta = 10$); then PPO + KL to that actor.
\item \textbf{IQL + HUBL $\to$ PPO + KL} (Geng et al., 2023). Same, with HUBL's relabelled targets
  $\tilde r = r + \gamma\lambda h'$, $\tilde\gamma = \gamma(1-\lambda)$, $h$ the Monte-Carlo return-to-go and
  $\lambda = 0.1\cdot\mathrm{rank}(\tau)/n$ per trajectory.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Protocol.} 5 seeds per method and experiment, 1.5M PPO steps. The heuristic's offline data
(64 episodes, i.e.\ 12,800 steps; 6,400 for inventory) is counted as environment interaction; HEPO's heuristic
half is part of its PPO steps. Policies are evaluated greedily every 10 updates on 64 fixed episodes (learning
curves) and at the end on 256 fixed episodes, the same for every method and heuristic. Method-specific
hyperparameters were set once from the papers' recommendations and not tuned.

the final metric (Q1, Q3) and Table~\ref{tab:beat} the steps to beat the guide (Q2). Figures~\ref{fig:overview}

\paragraph{Q1: better than RL from scratch?} Rarely, with 1.5M steps.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Only two cases are significantly better than PPO from scratch: IQL $\to$ PPO + KL on bin packing with Sum of
  Squares (1.51\% vs 2.48\%) and HuRL on load balancing (1.62 vs 1.64).
\item Most other cases are within noise. On bin packing PPO from scratch varies a lot between seeds
  ($\pm$0.85\%), so even large mean gains (BC $\to$ PPO + KL 1.63\% vs 2.48\% with Best Fit) are not significant
  with 5 seeds.
\item Several are significantly \emph{worse}: on knapsack and load balancing, PPO from scratch ends at 1.45\% and
  1.64, and most warm starts end at 1.56--1.82\% and 1.65--1.69. Starting from the heuristic, and especially being
  anchored to it, can leave the policy short of what PPO finds alone.
\item HEPO is significantly worse than PPO from scratch in four experiments: it ends at the guide's level
  (Q3 below).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Q2: reaches the heuristic's level faster?} Yes, for most methods that use the heuristic offline, as long
as the guide is beatable.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item IQL $\to$ PPO + KL, IQL + HUBL $\to$ PPO + KL, DAgger $\to$ PPO and BC $\to$ HuRL are faster than PPO from
  scratch in all five experiments; BC $\to$ PPO (naive or + KL) in four.
\item With a strong guide the gain is large: 13k steps instead of 205k on load balancing, 6k instead of 102k on
  inventory, and 13--74k for most warm starts instead of 1157k on bin packing with Sum of Squares (where PPO from
  scratch beats the guide in only 4 of 5 seeds).
\item With the weak Best Fit guide, cloning is \emph{slower} (BC $\to$ PPO 812k, BC $\to$ PPO + KL 771k, vs
  573k): the clone starts exactly at Best Fit's level and has to unlearn it, and the KL anchor resists that. Methods that change the learning problem instead are faster:
  HuRL from scratch 156k, BC $\to$ HuRL 218k.
\item HuRL from scratch is the least reliable here (faster in two of five): its shortened horizon helps on bin
  packing with Best Fit and on knapsack, but not on load balancing (504k vs 205k).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Q3: better than the heuristic?} Yes, for every warm-started method; but this does not separate the
methods from PPO from scratch, which beats the heuristic in four of five experiments too.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item All warm-started methods end significantly better than the guide in all five experiments, and HuRL in four
  (with Sum of Squares it is within noise: 1.99 $\pm$ 0.38\% vs 2.36\%).
\item PPO from scratch also does, except on bin packing with Sum of Squares, where it is within noise of the guide
  (2.48\% vs 2.36\%).
\item HEPO stays at the guide's level on bin packing (9.68\% $\pm$ 0.00 with Best Fit, 2.40\% with Sum of Squares).
  Its multiplier $\alpha$ keeps growing because the sampled (exploring) policy rarely beats the deterministic
  heuristic in our Monte-Carlo estimate, and the heuristic's half of the data keeps pulling $\pi$ towards
  $\pi_H$. We attribute this to our adaptation rather than to HEPO itself.
\item How far beyond: on bin packing the warm starts end 12--36\% below Sum of Squares' waste and 62--83\% below
  Best Fit's; on knapsack the best methods halve the static threshold's gap (1.44\% vs 3.69\%) but none reaches
  the adaptive threshold (1.24\%); on inventory all methods reach the level of capped base stock (6.68--6.75 vs
  6.70).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Q2 is where the heuristic pays off.} Offline use of a strong heuristic (IQL/HUBL, DAgger, BC)
  passes it 10--20 times sooner than PPO from scratch (load balancing, inventory, bin packing with Sum of Squares);
  IQL/HUBL $\to$ PPO + KL, DAgger $\to$ PPO and BC $\to$ HuRL are faster in every experiment, though only
  marginally with the weak Best Fit guide for IQL/HUBL.
\item \textbf{Q1 is rarely answered yes} with a budget at which PPO from scratch converges: only IQL $\to$ PPO + KL
  (bin packing, strong guide) and HuRL (load balancing) end significantly better, and anchored warm starts can end
  worse. A smaller budget is the natural test of whether the lead from Q2 turns into a final advantage.
\item \textbf{Q3 is easy on these problems}: nearly every method, including PPO from scratch, ends beyond the guide;
  the stronger references (adaptive threshold, capped base stock) are matched at best.
\item With a \textbf{weak guide}, cloning it does not even speed things up; reward shaping with its value (HuRL)
  does.
\item \textbf{HEPO}, as adapted to a heuristic policy, does not get past the guide on bin packing; a fairer version
  would compare greedy returns in the multiplier update or leave the heuristic's half out of the policy update.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations.} 5 seeds (so only large differences are significant), one training budget, untuned
method-specific hyperparameters, and small problems whose guides are fully observable functions of the state (so
cloning is almost exact). Q2 uses the first evaluation below the guide on 64 episodes, which is noisy for methods
that start right at the guide's level.
